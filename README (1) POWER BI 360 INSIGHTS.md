### Project Overview 
AltiQ Hardware, a fast-growing global company, sells computers and accessories through retailers, direct sales, and distributors. After opening a new store in America, the company faced unexpected losses due to decisions based on surveys, gut feeling, and basic Excel analysis.

To stay competitive with rivals using advanced analytics, AltiQ decided to adopt Power BI to bring data-driven decision-making into its business. The goal was to provide clear, actionable insights across finance, sales, marketing, and supply chain teams.

I contributed to this project by applying skills from the Codebasics Power BI Course. I helped build interactive dashboards and reports that gave stakeholders a better understanding of business performance and supported smarter decisions. ✅

📊 Understanding the Dataset

"Before performing any analysis, gaining clarity on the datasets is necessary. This project involves two data tables:"
 The project uses dimension tables for static data like customers and products, and fact tables for transactional insights.

From the gdb041 schema:

dim_customer, dim_market, dim_product: Static reference data.

fact_forecast_monthly: Stores forecasted monthly demand, enabling better inventory planning and cost savings.

fact_sales_monthly: Records actual monthly sales, differing only in the final column containing sold quantities.

From the gdb056 schema:

Key financial metrics are captured in: freight_cost, gross_price, manufacturing_cost, pre_invoice_deductions, and post_invoice_deductions.

### 💼 Essential Business Terms

Gross Sales: Total sales value before any deductions — your top-line revenue.

Gross Sales %: Share of gross sales compared to a total or target — shows sales strength.

Gross Margin (GM): Revenue left after deducting COGS — a direct indicator of profitability.

🔹Gross Margin %: GM as a percentage of revenue — the higher, the better your efficiency.

🔹Pre-Invoice Deductions: Adjustments made before the invoice (like early payment discounts).

🔹Post-Invoice Deductions: Adjustments made after the invoice (returns, rebates, etc.).

🔹Net Sales: Gross sales minus deductions — the actual revenue earned.

🔹Net Invoice Sales: The final invoiced amount, reflecting both pre and post-invoice adjustments.

🔹Net Profit: What’s left after all costs and expenses — your bottom line.

🔹Net Profit %: A profitability ratio showing how much of every dollar turns into profit.

🔹COGS (Cost of Goods Sold): The direct costs to produce goods — the core of cost efficiency.

🔹YTD (Year-to-Date): Cumulative performance from the start of the year to now.

🔹YTG (Year-to-Go): What’s left of the year — the opportunity still on the table.

🔹Direct: Sales straight from the company to the customer — no middlemen.

🔹Retailer: Businesses that sell products to end-users — like stores or online platforms.

🔹Consumer: The end-user of the product — the one who actually uses it.

🔹Distributor: Middle entities that buy in bulk and resell to retailers or direct customers.
